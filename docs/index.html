01
The idea

Provenance by input modality.

Most "AI detection" guesses after the fact. Margin records at the source: provenance is captured by how the text enters the document, not by a label you add later. Four origins, tracked as you write.

Typed

You typed it. This is yours, the gold standard.

AI

Generated in the app, or by a coding agent. Carries the prompt that produced it.

Source

Pasted as a quote. Carries a citation.

Imported

A third-party doc you built on. Marked "not mine."

Anything you didn't type enters as an atomic span you can't quietly edit into your own. To make it yours, you retype it, or you claim it with a reason. AI text is never free.

03
The app

Write first. Everything else gets out of the way.

A clean, Typora-style writing surface. Provenance is a quiet tint while you write and a full record when you export. Records carry a fingerprint, and a client-side verifier flags any edit after signing.

The editor. Text on the page, no panels. AI passages a soft blue.
The record. Composition bar, endnotes, fingerprint.
Own it. Claim a reworked AI passage with a reason. Origin stays; framing changes.
Verify. Recompute the fingerprint in the browser. Catches edits after signing.
04
Lineage

Built on coauthor.

Margin didn't start from scratch. It builds on coauthor, a shared surface where a human and an AI agent edit the same document. coauthor defined a small format: feedback anchored to exact spans of text, kept in a sidecar so the document stays clean.

Margin reuses that exact format. A document's provenance lives in a .coauthor sidecar, the same anchored-metadata file coauthor reads. So provenance and the agent's feedback loop sit in one file, with no conversion: an agent can review a Margin document, and a coauthor document can carry provenance.

coauthor solved

  • How a human and an agent shape a document together: anchored comments, version history, a clean sidecar.

  • And remember who shaped which part: the same sidecar now also carries the origin of every span.
05
Agents

Let a coding agent write, and keep the receipts.

Point Claude Code at your workspace. It edits through a CLI and an MCP server, so every change it makes is recorded as AI-attributed provenance, carrying the prompt that caused it. The edit appears in your open editor live.

# tighten paragraph three, and keep the provenance
margin revise weekly-note \
  --find "then we fixed it" \
  --prompt "say what we actually did, concrete" \
  --text "The fix shipped Thursday behind a flag..."

# the rewrite lands in the editor, marked AI, with its prompt
06
Honest

What it is, and what it isn't.

What it is

  • Good-faith "show your work." A record you sign, the way you'd show your work in math class.
  • Author-side. The value is for the writer and a trusted team, where the record reaches people who'll actually open it.
  • Honest about AI. Use the tool. Own the outcome. Disclosure becomes an owned account, not a confession.

What it isn't

  • Not DRM. The fingerprint detects edits to a record. It does not prove the typed words are original.
  • Not a detector. It records at the source. It doesn't guess whether someone else used AI.
  • Not a market bet. A concept app, built and used. The thinking is the point.
